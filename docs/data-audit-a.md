# Independent source audit: batch A

Audited 2026-11-12 by the agent that did not author batch A. Scope: chicken wings; all three chicken-breast sizes; turkey breast; steak; pork chops; salmon; scallops; carrots. Ten variants across eight foods were checked against the actual linked primary pages. No data file was edited in this audit.

## Result

The recorded cooking temperatures and numerical time intervals match the cited primary pages. Preparation quantities are supported, and the existing thermometer instructions are more conservative than several source authors' culinary stopping advice. Those source differences must be made explicit rather than implying that the reference clock was validated to the site's official safety endpoint. This was a source audit, not a physical cooking test.

## Required safety-context fixes

1. **Steak: disclose the reference's carryover stopping rule.** The [Skinnytaste recipe](https://www.skinnytaste.com/air-fryer-steak/) supports the 400°F/12-minute medium option, four 1-inch sirloin steaks totaling 1½ lb, halfway turn and five-minute rest. However, its doneness chart lists medium at 145°F while telling readers to remove steaks five degrees before the target. Existing notes mention rarer options but omit this medium-method difference. Add a note and source-evidence context that the reference allows early removal, while this site requires at least 145°F before removal and the stated rest. The time must remain an estimate/checkpoint, not a promise of a safe medium finish.

2. **Scallops: identify the source's actual lower culinary target.** [Cooked by Julie](https://www.cookedbyjulie.com/air-fryer-scallops/) supports ½ lb sea scallops, 400°F, six minutes and a turn at three minutes. Its doneness FAQ specifies approximately 125–130°F, alongside opaque/firm appearance. The current note vaguely mentions lower-temperature finishes. Make the discrepancy explicit: the six-minute reference is not separately verified to a different thermometer target; the site's official shellfish appearance guidance controls serving. Keep sea scallops separate from much smaller bay scallops, which the reference times differently.

3. **Pork chops: make the source's internal inconsistency visible.** [Wholesome Yum](https://www.wholesomeyum.com/air-fryer-pork-chops/) has a recipe card supporting four 8 oz chops, five-minute preheat at 400°F and about twelve minutes, turning halfway, until 145°F. Its surrounding tips instead recommend removing at 140–143°F and using carryover; its thickness table is also framed around a 140°F probe. Keep the card-based setting and the site's at-least-145°F-before-removal instruction plus minimum three-minute rest. Add a note that the lower carryover advice is not adopted. Its card does not give exact thickness, so the existing caution about atypical thickness should stay.

4. **Salmon: retain the existing stricter rule and make clock interpretation explicit.** [Well Plated](https://www.wellplated.com/air-fryer-salmon/) supports two roughly 6 oz fillets, preheat 400°F, six to eleven minutes, with one-inch fillets usually eight to nine minutes. It also recommends taking fish out five to ten degrees before 145°F to allow carryover. The existing data already says this site does not assume that rise, so no numeric change is necessary. Keep that note prominent and characterize the time as a checking interval, because reaching 145°F before removal may take longer.

The relevant official comparison is the [FoodSafety.gov minimum-temperature chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures): poultry 165°F; whole beef/pork 145°F plus three minutes' rest; fish 145°F (or its stated appearance alternative); scallops milky/pearly or white and opaque. The site's visible safe finish should control if a culinary recipe differs.
